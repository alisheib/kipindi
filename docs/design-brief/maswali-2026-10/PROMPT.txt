2 · THE GAME: the owner's rulings of 2026-10-16, exact
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
These replace R1's rules, and the management proposal wherever they differ. Draw them exactly.

 1. PRICE. A ticket costs TZS 1,000 and is paid from the MAIN balance only ("salio kuu"). Bonus money
    is refused with a message that names the reason.
 2. QUESTIONS. Each week publishes 15 Yes/No questions: 10 MAIN, numbered 1–10; 5 RESERVE, shown as
    A1–A5. (The owner's rulings call the reserves R1–R5; on screen they are "A" for Akiba — a draft
    label, flag it in DECISIONS.md.) The player answers ALL 15 before paying.
 3. VOID. A question is void when it cannot be resolved: a match abandoned or postponed past its
    deadline, no clear outcome, or no reading from its source.
    - ONE officer records each result and each void. An AI may prepare evidence and a suggestion; it
      never seals a result.
 4. RESERVES. A reserve counts ONLY when a main question voids.
    - The voided mains are filled by the first unused NON-VOID reserves, in the order A1 → A5.
    - A voided reserve is skipped.
    - The lowest-numbered voided main takes the first such reserve, and so on (owner's ruling). The
      pairing never changes who wins — the graded set is the same either way — it fixes what is drawn.
 5. WEEK VOID. If the voided mains outnumber the non-void reserves, the WHOLE WEEK voids and every
    ticket is refunded in full; the money carried in from earlier weeks carries forward again in full.
    - The moment that becomes certain, sales stop (if still open) and the week band reads
      "Wiki inabatilishwa" with the refund time. Refunds are paid when the 24-hour check window on the
      voids that caused it closes (an upheld objection can still reinstate a question until then). Then
      the band reads "Wiki imebatilishwa".
 6. WIN. A ticket wins when all 10 graded positions match. A graded position is a main, or the reserve
    carried into a voided main's place. 9 of 10 wins nothing, except in the SUNSET week (rule 15).
    There are no tiers.
 7. JACKPOT. Jackpot = the larger of TZS 1,000,000 (the guaranteed floor) and the pool (the rollover
    carried in plus this week's ticket sales). The floor counts BEFORE the fee.
 8. FEE AND SHARE, in a week with at least one winning ticket:
    - 13% of the jackpot is the platform fee. The TRA 10% and GBT 5% levies are booked from that fee
      when it is taken. A player never pays them.
    - The other 87% is shared EQUALLY PER WINNING TICKET (two winning tickets get two shares), each
      share floored to the whole shilling.
    - If the pool is below the floor, the house tops up the gap — only in a winning week, never
      carried over.
    - A rounding remainder from the equal split is never paid from house money: in a week above the
      floor it stays in the jackpot for next week; in a floor week the top-up is reduced by it.
    Example: 1,050,000 − 136,500 fee = 913,500 to winners ÷ 3 tickets = 304,500 each.
    Floor week: 1,000,000 − 130,000 fee = 870,000 to winners.
 9. NO WINNER. No fee is taken. 100% of the pool rolls into next week, with no cap, until someone wins.
    The floor and any top-up never roll.
10. CAP. At most 10 tickets per player per week, printed on the slip. A ticket cancelled in its free
    window does not count toward the 10.
11. FREE CANCEL. A ticket can be cancelled for a full refund within 5 minutes of purchase, but only if
    sales still had at least 5 more minutes to run when it was bought (the platform's existing rule,
    measured to the sales close).
12. WEEK SCHEDULE.
    - Sales open Monday and close Friday 18:00 EAT. Events run Friday evening to Sunday.
    - After the last result is final, a 24-hour check window runs (it never closes before 12:00 EAT)
      while players may dispute a result; a different officer rules on disputes. Payout follows,
      normally Monday or Tuesday.
    - An officer may move the close; the new time replaces the old one everywhere, and one neutral
      notice says so. The system REFUSES any question whose event starts before the sales close.
    - The next week opens on its own schedule. Its jackpot includes last week's rollover once last week
      settles. Until then the new week's figure is a true LOWER BOUND, labelled "angalau" (at least).
13. INSIDERS. Staff (any admin role) and house or desk accounts cannot buy; they get a clear refusal.
    House bots never touch Millionea.
14. CATEGORIES. Use ONLY the platform's closed category list and its existing labels: Michezo (sports) ·
    Uchumi (macro: stocks and FX) · Hali ya hewa (weather) · Kripto (crypto) · Utamaduni (culture:
    entertainment) · Teknolojia (tech) · Nyingine (other). There is NO politics category, ever.
15. SUNSET. If the product is ever stopped for good, the final week is announced at least one full week
    before its sales open, runs normally, and the unwon pool goes to that final week's highest-scoring
    tickets, shared equally.
    - In that final week ONLY, a score decides who is paid, so its settlement states the winning score.
      Nowhere else does a score appear.
    - The 13% fee applies to the sunset payout. There is no floor top-up unless a ticket got 10/10.
16. REGISTER. The pool is held in its own house ledger account as money owed to players. A public
    register shows every question, result, void, substitution and ledger line, with the attesting
    officer's ROLE (two roles if the platform's optional two-officer flag is on).

5 · THE CONCEPT: "DAFTARI NA MUHURI" (THE REGISTER AND THE SEAL)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
In one line: every ticket is an entry in the week's public register. Every result is stamped onto it
along the mark's own −14° axis. Every shilling is a line in a ledger. Only ten matching marks in an
unbroken line open the Seal.

"Daftari" means both the school exercise book a teacher marks with ticks and crosses, and the official
register. The product should feel as familiar as the first and as official as the second. The metaphor
is ART DIRECTION ONLY; player copy uses plain dictionary words (tiketi, bwawa, akiba, batili, "Daftari
la Wiki").

FIVE OBJECTS, all built from existing parts:
 1. THE TICKET: a document with a stub. Fifteen numbered clauses — mains 1–10 on the flat rung, then
    reserves A1–A5 on .mat-inset below a ruled divider whose heading states the reserve rule. Under a
    perforated rule sits the stub: serial · week · TZS 1,000 · purchase time · the question-set hash,
    short and labelled. The perforation is the ticket glyph's own notch line, drawn at document scale.
 2. THE SEAL LINE: the instrument. Ten sockets in a row, with five smaller annex sockets beneath. Every
    socket is a member of the r 8.6 status-ring family. Sizes: 24 on the ticket, 18 in lists, 14 in rows.
 3. THE LEDGER: the money. The jackpot is never a hero number: one line in a short public ledger, mono,
    neutral ink, with the headline rule beside it.
 4. THE WEEK STRIP: three static phases in plain words, nothing ticking: "Mauzo · Jumatatu – Ijumaa
    18:00 EAT" · "Matukio · Ijumaa jioni – Jumapili" · "Matokeo na malipo · Jumatatu/Jumanne".
 5. THE SEAL: the existing Struck Seal, unchanged except that its amount does not count. Reached only
    through an unbroken line of ten matches, and only on the player's tap or LIVE.

SOCKET GRAMMAR: four shapes in one ring, each with a word wherever there is room:
 · Awaiting the event:  clockPending · --text-muted · "Inasubiri" / "Awaiting".
 · Pick matched:        sealCheck · --success-fg (never --yes-*) · "Imelingana" / "Matched".
 · Did not match:       sealX (voidX geometry, renamed) · --text-muted (never rose) · "Haikulingana" / "No match".
 · Not counted:         sealNil (NEW): a ring with a level bar, the accountant's nil · --text-muted ·
                        "Batili" / "Haikutumika". Covers a voided main, a voided reserve and an unused
                        reserve. Must not read as a no-entry sign or a minus at 14px; prove it.
 · Reserve standing by: clockPending at annex size · "Akiba".
NEVER let a cross mean "void". In Tanzanian school marking a cross means WRONG, so a void drawn with a
cross reads as a loss and breaks the reserve rule in the player's head.

HOW THE RULES ARE TAUGHT, without a paragraph
 · 15 picks up front: the ticket has fifteen clauses; the sticky rail reads "Kuu 7/10 · Akiba 2/5";
   the slip's Endelea arms only at 15/15.
 · Reserves count only for a voided main, in order: annex sockets stay "Akiba" whatever their own event
   does; a reserve becomes a mark only after it CARRIES into the vacated main socket, and the annex then
   reads "→4"; a voided reserve shows sealNil "Batili" and is visibly passed over.
 · Week void: a live line reads "Akiba halali zilizobaki: 4 kati ya 5". When the count would go below
   zero the week band reads "Wiki inabatilishwa" (refund time shown), then "Wiki imebatilishwa", and
   every stub rejoins.
 · Floor and rollover: the ledger shows "Jumla ya bwawa" and "Dhamana ya chini"; the Jackpot line repeats
   the larger; when the pool passes the floor, the floor line goes muted but stays visible.
 · Fee at payout only: the fee is in the headline rule everywhere; the ledger's fee line reads "—" until a
   payout; rollover rows print "ada 0".
 · Equal share per ticket: "÷ tiketi 3 zilizoshinda", then "× 2" when the player holds two.
 · Close before the first event: slip and hub print "Mauzo yanafungwa: Ijumaa 18:00" over "Tukio la
   kwanza: Ijumaa 19:30".
