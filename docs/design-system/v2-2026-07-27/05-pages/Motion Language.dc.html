50pick · Motion identity

The Settle

Our mark is a needle on a pivot, poised between two outcomes. That is also how the product moves. Everything swings in from a real anchor, decelerates hard, overshoots by a hair, and settles — the way a balance finds true. Nothing floats in from nowhere. Nothing bounces twice. A market either tips or it doesn't.

01 · Curves

Four curves. No fifth.

A product that uses ten easings has none. Every motion in 50pick is one of these four, and the choice is decided by what happened, not by taste.

Springs are deliberately absent. A spring's rest time depends on displacement, so identical UI settles at different moments — on a market screen that reads as instability. Our overshoot is baked into the curve: fixed, tiny, repeatable, and free to run on the compositor.

02 · Duration

Time is a function of distance and consequence

Two inputs decide every duration, so nobody ever picks a number by feel. Distance sets the base tier. Consequence — how much of the player's money the change concerns — moves it one step up.

{{ d.ms }} {{ d.use }} {{ d.token }}

Money rule: any transition that shows, commits, or settles player funds takes the next tier up and never uses --m-leave.
Ceiling: nothing in the product exceeds 620ms. If it needs longer, it is a page, not a transition.

03 · The signature

Everything reveals along the needle's axis — −14°

This is the fingerprint. Where other products wipe horizontally or fade in place, our edge always travels on the same tilt as the divider in the mark. Skeletons sweep on it, panels reveal along it, settled results seal across it, charts draw under it. One angle, used everywhere, becomes unmistakable — and it costs nothing, because it is the same GPU transform either way.

Axis sweep · skeletons, gilt shine

04 · Depth

Four planes, and blur is the only depth cue that moves

Z-order is a fixed ladder, not a per-component decision. When a plane opens above another, the layer beneath does not scale, dim or slide — it takes a small, cheap blur and loses a little contrast. That reads as depth on any device and costs one filter on one element.
