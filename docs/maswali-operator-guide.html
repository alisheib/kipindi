Maswali Millionea — Operator Guide

For 50pick staff who run Maswali Millionea, the weekly jackpot. No technical knowledge required.

NOT ON SALE YET Millionea is built and switched off. No player can see it until the Owner moves its rollout to Everyone, and no week can open until the Owner has recorded the licence basis and funded the guarantee. Until then this guide describes the console, not anything a player has met.

Every figure below is the rules’ default. Each is an admin setting, and every week freezes its own figures the moment it opens — the week’s page always shows that week’s. Where this guide and a week’s page disagree, the page is right.

Operating rules — read before you begin

A week’s rules freeze the moment it opens. The ticket price, the floor, the fee, the ticket cap, the void deadlines, the check window, the fifteen questions and their sources are fixed when an officer presses Open the week. Nothing changes them afterwards; a change to the settings applies from the next week. The one thing that can still move is the close, within its bounds, while the week sells (section 6). If a player disputes a figure, read the figures on their week’s page — never today’s settings.
A week is fifteen questions: ten mains and five reserves. A ticket answers all fifteen, YES or NO. Only ten are graded, and a ticket wins only on 10 of 10. When a main voids, the first usable reserve, in order, takes its place. If more mains void than there are usable reserves, the whole week voids and every ticket is refunded in full.
Only an officer seals a result — never the AI. The AI may attach evidence and a suggestion; the seal is an officer’s act, with the evidence written down. Seal what the source says, and only that.
The seal console shows no ticket data, by design. No picks, no holders, nothing about what a result would cost. An officer who could see which answer pays whom could be steered. The seal log on the integrity page shows the record only once a week’s results can no longer change.
Seal all fifteen. Reserves are sealed in order, all fifteen by default. A correction that voids a main later, inside the check window, can pull an unused reserve in, and that reserve must already carry its result.
A void has a ground from a closed list, and evidence. Six grounds (section 7), each printed on the week’s public register. There is no “management may refund” ground on Millionea, and a fault on 50pick’s side is never a ground for voiding one question. A whole-week void for such a fault is not built yet (OD-26): report a fault to engineering and Compliance at once.
The check window: 24 hours, never closing before 12:00 EAT. It opens when the results are final. Only the officer who sealed a question may correct it, and never after the window closes — the console offers Correct… while the window is open — with its evidence (at least ten characters, as a seal). A corrected result is confirmed by a second officer whenever the original seal needed one, and a correction starts the window again.
One accounting officer releases the money, from figures prepared in advance. The release pays exactly the figures shown, in one transaction. If anything changed since they were prepared, it refuses and you read the new figures. An alert fires when a week’s results are final, its window has closed and it is still not released 4 hours later.
The guarantee backs the floor before a week can open. It must hold the floor, less any confirmed carry-over, for every unsettled week — the new one included. Week N+1 opens while week N is still being graded, so before week 1 the Owner pays in TZS 2,000,000: two floors.
Pause, void and stop are three different acts. A pause stops new tickets and nothing else — settlements, refunds and cancellations go on. A whole-week void refunds every ticket and leaves the carried jackpot where it is. Stopping the product is the Owner’s final-week ceremony.
Staff never buy tickets. Accounts that hold a staff role, and accounts 50pick operates, are refused at the purchase.
Never tell a player which answers to choose. Not a hint, not “most people say”, not how many tickets to buy. Explain the rules and point to the page (section 11).

DO NOT Do not seal from memory or from the AI’s suggestion. Do not leave a fixture that moved earlier for later. Do not release a week whose figures you have not read. These are the three ways this game goes wrong, and all three look like saving time.

1 · What the game is

A player buys a ticket at a fixed price and answers fifteen YES/NO questions about the coming weekend’s events. Every ticket that gets all ten graded questions right shares the week’s jackpot equally. If nobody does, the whole week’s sales roll into the next jackpot.

1 · DraftA trading officer writes the fifteen questions in English, Swahili and Chinese — the Wednesday before.
2 · Attest and openA second reader confirms the three languages agree; the week opens on its Monday, 00:00 EAT.
3 · SalesPlayers buy, up to 10 tickets each a week, until Friday 18:00 EAT.
4 · CloseSales stop on their own. The tickets’ fingerprint is stamped and published on /fairness.
5 · SealAn officer seals each result from its source as the events finish, Friday evening to Monday.
6 · Check and releaseA 24-hour check window, then one accounting officer releases the money.
J, the jackpot = the larger of the floor (TZS 1,000,000) and the carry-over plus the week’s sales F, our fee = 13% of J — only in a week where at least one ticket has 10 of 10 each share = (J − F) ÷ the number of winning tickets, rounded down to the shilling the remainder = under one shilling per winner — it joins the next jackpot as far as the week’s own money covers it; the rest stays with the guarantee (all of it, in a topped-up week), whose top-up pays only the fee and the shares; in the final week, the part the week’s own money covers stays in the jackpot account the top-up = what the guarantee adds so the jackpot is paid in full — only in a week with a winner no winner = the whole week’s sales roll to the next jackpot, and no fee is taken TRA and GBT = 10% and 5% of our fee — never of a player’s share

THE ONE SURPRISE A winning ticket can be paid less than it cost. Every winner gets the same share, and there is no minimum share: if very many tickets reach 10 of 10, each share is small. On the polls and Up & Down a winning bet is never paid less than its stake; on Millionea it can be. Say so plainly when asked.

4 · Drafting a week

On Millionea, press Draft a week. Choose the Monday the week starts (the next one by default) and its line: Public, or Drive — a private line for our own test weeks, which never feeds a public jackpot. The sales open and close on the rules’ template days.

Start from the calendar lists the fixtures from the event calendar and the AI’s draft polls that fall in the week’s window. Choosing one fills a slot with its words, its source and its time; nothing is published, and every row still has to pass the checks below.

4.1 · Every field, for each of the fifteen slots (M1–M10, then A1–A5)

FieldWhat to enter
Category · kind of eventSport, a price, the weather or an announcement. The kind picks the wording template for the criterion.
Question — English, Kiswahili, 中文One plain YES/NO question in each language. The counter beside it is a guide for the player’s screen, not a limit.
How it resolves — three languagesThe criterion, exactly as the source will settle it. Use the template fills the English; replace every {…} — a criterion still carrying one is not finished.
Event date, start time, the source’s zoneTyped as the source prints it, in its own zone — a Premier League kickoff in London time. The week page shows it in that zone and in EAT. A start inside a clock change’s repeated hour is refused, never guessed.
Source · fallback sourceThe official result’s address, and a second one for when the first is down. Both must be on the trusted-source list.
Estimated chance of YES · where it comes fromA percentage between 30% and 70%, and its source. Questions that are near-certain do not belong on a ticket.
Reading toleranceFor a price or a reading only: how close counts, for example “two decimals, as published”.

4.2 · The checks before a week can open

The week page lists every reason the week cannot open, in words, and the open refuses until the list is empty:

  • exactly fifteen questions — ten mains and five reserves;
  • a second reader has attested that English, Swahili and Chinese agree;
  • the close is in the future, and at least 30 minutes before the earliest of the fifteen events;
  • every question is in a market category, and none is about politics — the licence excludes it;
  • every language has its question and its criterion;
  • each question’s void deadline falls inside the week’s final deadline;
  • each question carries its estimated chance, between 30% and 70%, and its source;
  • at most 4 questions from one competition, and the reserves from at least 3 different sources;
  • every source and fallback is trusted, and no two questions are about the same event;
  • beside the week itself: a licence basis is recorded, and the guarantee covers every unsettled week.

Edit the questions on the week page reopens the builder. Any edit clears the attestation, so the second reader reads again.

5 · Attest and open

  1. The second reader — never the officer who drafted the week — reads every question and criterion in all three languages, then presses I read all three languages: they agree.
  2. Open the week on its Monday at 00:00 EAT. The confirmation says it plainly: the rules, the fifteen questions and their sources freeze now and cannot change. Sales start at once for everyone the rollout admits.
  3. If the open refuses, its reason is in words. Fix it and open again.

One week sells at a time, and weeks settle strictly in calendar order. A second public week cannot open while one is selling, and a week cannot settle before the week before it has settled or voided.

6 · While it sells

6.1 · Moving the close

On the week page, Move the sales close. The platform refuses a move that breaks a rule, and says which:

  • earlier — never sooner than 6 minutes from now, so every ticket keeps its 5-minute free cancellation;
  • later — never past 30 minutes before the first event;
  • never once the close has passed.

6.2 · An event moved earlier

AT ONCE No ticket may ever be graded on a question whose event began before the ticket was bought. If a question’s event is brought forward: move the close to at or before the new start while that is still in the future. If tickets were already sold after the event actually began, seal that question VOID on the ground Settled or begun before the sales close (MR-15’s ground for this case) as soon as the seal console opens at the close, with the event’s real start as the evidence — or, if it is already sealed, its sealing officer corrects it to that VOID while the check window is open. There is no void control while the week is on sale. Its reserve then applies, and the register prints the ground as an event that had begun before the sales close.

6.3 · Pausing sales

Compliance can pause sales on the integrity page, with a written reason. No ticket can be bought until sales resume; tickets already bought are safe, and settlements, refunds and cancellations go on. A pause is not a void and not a stop.

7 · Sealing the results

Once a week closes, its page shows the seal console: every slot with its question, its criterion, its source, its event time and its void deadline. It shows nothing about the tickets.

  1. Seal YES or NO. For each result that has landed, choose YES or NO and write what the source says in Evidence — at least ten characters. Press Seal, check the list in the confirmation, type SEAL. Several results can be sealed together.
  2. Void a question. Void… on its row: choose the ground, write the evidence, type VOID. A voided main is replaced by the next usable reserve; a voided reserve is skipped.
  3. Correct a seal. Correct… appears while the check window is open, and only the officer who sealed the question may use it: the right result (YES, NO or VOID with its ground), the evidence, CORRECT. The window starts again.
  4. Confirm as second officer appears on a seal made while the platform-wide two-officer setting was on, until a second officer confirms it — and again after its correction, whatever the setting says by then.

The AI’s suggestion, where there is one, is shown under the question as AI suggests (never seals). Read the source yourself.

7.1 · The six grounds for a void

Ground, as the console names itWhen it applies
Not played by its void deadlineThe event was not played or not finished within 48 hours of its scheduled start (never later than the Monday 23:59 EAT after the close). The deadline is printed on the question.
Abandoned, no official resultThe event was abandoned and the source publishes no result.
No reading from the sourcesNeither the source nor the named fallback gave a reading by the deadline.
Settled or begun before the sales closeThe outcome was publicly known, or the event had already begun, before sales closed — an event brought forward that began while tickets were still sold is this ground (section 6.2). It may be sealed at any time.
Criterion wrong, or the texts divergeThe criterion is materially wrong, or the English and Swahili say different things; found before the close.
Criterion wrong, found after the closeThe same, found after the close and before the check window ends — the sealing officer’s own finding, or a player’s report through support. The register says only that it was found after the close. (Players’ formal per-question objections are not built yet.)

7.2 · Where the results stand

The week list’s Results column reads N of 15 sealed · needs … while slots are missing, Final · window to … once the results are final, or Voids whole when more mains voided than there are usable reserves. The week page’s Results tile says the same, slot by slot.

8 · Paying out

The pools page is Accounting’s. Its four tiles: Jackpot carried (players’ money, waiting for the next winning week), Guarantee (Owner capital, beside what the unsettled weeks need now), Payout float (beside the payouts already prepared) and Drive jackpot (the private line’s own). A balance that cannot be read says so; it is never shown as zero.

Each open week’s row shows its sales, the carry-over it has claimed, its coverage — how many of the 1,024 ways to answer the ten mains some ticket holds — the largest top-up it could need, and, once its window has closed, its prepared settlement: the winners, each share, the fee and the top-up, or the amount that rolls.

  1. Read the prepared figures. A week with a winner shows N winners · each share · fee · top-up; a week without one shows No 10 of 10: the sales roll to the jackpot.
  2. Press Release, type RELEASE. The release pays exactly those figures, in one transaction.
  3. If the figures changed since they were prepared, the release refuses — read the new figures and release again. If the guarantee cannot cover the top-up, the settlement stops: the Owner pays in, then release again. It never pays less.

A winner Compliance has flagged is paid and held for Compliance; the row says how many. The rollover trail lists every settled week: its sales, the carry-over in, the winners, the fee, the top-up, what was paid and what carried on.

10 · The Owner’s ceremonies

The Owner card on the pools page holds the four acts only the Owner performs.

CeremonyWhat it does
GuaranteePay in (FUND GUARANTEE) or Take back (RETURN GUARANTEE), with the amount and the bank or rail reference. It is Owner capital, committed — never counted as retained earnings. A return is refused if it would leave the unsettled weeks’ floors unbacked.
Licence basisThe Owner’s dated ruling, or the Gaming Board’s letter, and where it is kept (RECORD). None withdraws it, and no week can open while none is recorded.
RolloutNobody (withdrawn), Staff preview or Everyone, with the reason; type the state chosen. The environment’s own ceiling still applies — the effective state is the lower of the two. A public week sells only while the rollout is Everyone; a drive week sells from Staff preview up. Withdrawing hides the product — every Millionea page and door, except a holder’s own tickets — and it never deletes a ticket, and it never stops a settlement or a refund.
Stop the productChoose the final week and the reason (SUNSET MILLIONEA). Three cases, exactly: a drafted week may be made final at least a full week before its sales open, and from then on the hub and the home card name it; the week on sale becomes final at once; while no week is on sale and a week’s results are being decided (its sales closed, not yet settled), that week settles under its own rules — a week without a 10 of 10 rolls over as always — and the next week to open may be made final even at less than a full week’s notice, its buyers told when they buy. A final week never opens before its announced time. A week whose sales have closed never becomes final itself: its results are already being decided. Between weeks — no week on sale or being decided — a stop takes the first path, a full week ahead. The final week runs normally. If no ticket gets 10 of 10, the highest score shares the whole jackpot — the fee applies, with no floor and no top-up. After a final week that ran, no public week opens again.

11 · What to say to players

While the rollout is not Everyone, Millionea is not a product players have: do not discuss it.

They askYou say
“How does it work?”A ticket is fifteen YES/NO answers at a fixed price. Ten of the questions count; a ticket with all ten right shares the week’s jackpot equally with every other one — except in the final week before Millionea is retired, where, if no ticket has all ten right, the tickets with the highest score share it. The full rules are under Help → the Millionea rules.
“Which answers should I pick?”We never advise on answers. Each question shows how it will be settled and by which source.
“I won, but my share is less than my ticket cost.”Every winning ticket gets the same share of the jackpot, and there is no minimum share. When very many tickets reach 10 of 10, each share is smaller. The week’s page shows the jackpot, the fee, the number of winning tickets and the share.
“Can I cancel my ticket?”Within 5 minutes of buying it, for a full refund — and a ticket bought in the last 5 minutes before the close cannot be cancelled. After the close, nothing can be bought or cancelled.
“A question was voided.”The first usable reserve, in order, takes its place, and the week’s page shows which. If more questions void than there are reserves, the whole week is refunded in full.
“Nobody won. Where did the money go?”All of it rolls into the next jackpot. No fee is taken in a week nobody wins. The one exception is the final week before Millionea is retired: there the tickets with the highest score share the jackpot, and the fee is taken.
“How do I know it is fair?”The questions’ fingerprint is published on /fairness when the week opens, and every ticket’s fingerprint is fixed when sales close. A ticket’s owner can check their own ticket from its page.
“When am I paid?”Once the results are final and the 24-hour check window has closed — usually Monday or Tuesday. The share goes straight to the wallet. Withdrawing needs a verified identity and a confirmed email, and carries the usual withdrawal fee.
“My winnings are on hold.”Our compliance team is reviewing the ticket. Hand it to Compliance; do not guess at the reason.
“A result is wrong.”Note the question and what the player says its source shows, and pass it to the trading officer on duty the same day. Inside the check window the officer who sealed the question can correct it, and the window starts again. Once the window has closed, the result stands.
