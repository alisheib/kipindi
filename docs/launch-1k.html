Railway's last four bills were $5.00, $11.15, $25.42 and $17.38 so far this period. The cost is rising because the platform is doing more work, not because anything is wrong.

Two facts that decide where the money goes.
The AI bill is bigger than the hosting bill — $50.75 against $31.81 — and 99.98% of it does not depend on customer numbers. It is the cost of creating and settling markets on a clock. Going from 104 customers to 1,000 will barely change it.
Memory is 87% of the 50pick hosting bill — $20.60 of $23.57. The application uses almost no processing power and holds 4.7 GB of memory. Capping that is the cheapest saving we have.

3 · What must be done before we open, and why

ItemCostReason
Turn on SMS $35–45/mo No member of the public can register today. Sign-up requires a code by text message; the platform is set to write those codes to a log file instead of sending them. It has never sent one. Needs a signed Selcom contract and a TCRA sender ID — a commercial step, not engineering.
Two-factor on the admin console $0 Nine administrator accounts control customer money and are protected by a password only. Second-factor was switched off and was meant to be restored on 31 July.
Health check on deployments $0 Railway is not checking whether a new version works before sending customers to it. A broken release goes live unchallenged. The status page also reports "healthy" while the database is down, so it must be fixed before it can be trusted.
Continuous database backup ~$2/mo We back up once a night. A failure at the wrong hour loses up to 24 hours of bets, deposits and payouts. Railway provides point-in-time recovery on our existing plan; it is not switched on.
Alerts that arrive by themselves $0 In August the nightly backup failed eleven nights running. The warning was displayed correctly for ten of them and nobody saw it. Today the same failure would repeat the same way.
Updates without downtime $0 Every release briefly takes the site down and cancels any bet being placed at that moment. At five customers a day nobody notices. At a thousand, everybody does.
Payouts on large markets $0 Winners are paid one at a time inside a thirty-second limit. Measured, that runs out at about 660 winners. Our biggest market ever had 29 bets, so it has never been reached — one popular football match with a thousand customers will pass it, and the market then freezes for betting and cashing out.
Cache the busy screens $2–4/mo Nothing is cached. Every page is rebuilt from the database on every view, and the main screens refresh themselves every 15–20 seconds. At 200 customers online that is roughly 400 database queries a second to redraw screens that have barely changed.
Move servers to Amsterdam $0 Servers are in California; customers are in Tanzania. The server answers in 31 milliseconds but a customer waits about half a second. That is distance, not slow software. Moving halves it and costs nothing. Requires one short planned outage.
Enlarge the database disk $0 now The disk is 897 MB of 5 GB and grows 14 MB a day — about six months left. The compliance record inside it must be kept seven years by law and cannot be deleted. Railway charges for space used, not reserved, so enlarging it costs nothing until it is filled.
Recovery procedure and rehearsal $0 There is no written procedure for restoring the platform, and no measured recovery time. The restore tool was rehearsed once in July against a database a tenth of today's size.
Two of these are decisions, not engineering. Turning on SMS needs a signed contract. Two-factor needs an administrator to enrol an authenticator app. Everything else is a few days of work and buys no new infrastructure.

Two figures in our own internal notes were wrong, both in our favour. The code and documentation state that the database allows 100 simultaneous connections and that Redis is switched off in production. Measured live: the database allows 500, and Redis is connected and working across containers. Several "we cannot scale" conclusions written earlier this year rested on those two stale figures and no longer hold.

Money safety — built, tested, and not to be rebuilt

  • Maintenance mode pauses new bets and deposits while leaving withdrawals open, so customer funds are never trapped by an operational decision.
  • Payment kill switches per mobile network, separately for money in and money out.
  • Deposits are settled from a signed re-query, not the incoming message, exactly once, defended against a tampered amount, and recovered by a 15-second poller and a 5-minute sweep even if the provider's confirmation never arrives.
  • A dead Redis cannot break a bet — proven by a test that points the platform at a dead port and asserts betting still succeeds.
  • A retry without an idempotency key is refused, so a retry can never become a second bet.
  • 200 simultaneous bets on one market: zero shillings lost or created.
  • The Up & Down safety net is independent of the game's own off switch — turning the game off must not disable the thing that returns money already staked in it.
  • Backups are verified by actually restoring them nightly into a scratch database and re-checking the money totals and the audit chain — not by confirming a file was written.
