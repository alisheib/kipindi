The House Desk

A guide for admins · 50pick

The desk lets the house take part in its own markets from ordinary player accounts, under limits you set. This guide explains what every control does, what to set it to, and what to check when an account is not betting.

The one rule that matters most

An account only bets when every condition is satisfied at once: the master switch is on, the account is Active, its core limits are filled in, it has money, and its rules point at markets that actually exist. If any one of those is missing, it stays silent — and that is by design.

1 · Getting an account betting

Do these in order. Each step is a screen on the desk.

Designate an account. Pick an existing player account the desk will stake from, and confirm you have the holder's permission. The desk never creates accounts of its own. Search for it by handle, phone number or account ID — or, if you have none of those, pick it from the list of every account underneath the search: twenty to a page, sortable by account, joined and signed in, and it says why an account cannot be chosen.
Fill in the core money limits. On the Limits tab. An empty core limit means "refuse", not "no limit", so a blank core box stops the account betting. Leave either optional two-sided desk budget empty to keep a second desk account off the opposite side. Press Save on Limits before leaving this tab.
Choose the markets. On the Rules tab, under Markets: tick Up & Down and/or Polls, then tick at least one chain or category underneath. Ticking a product without ticking anything under it reaches no market at all.
Choose how it enters. Tick Counter, Fill or Opener for each product you enabled, as explained in section 2. An Up & Down account used only for pairing can instead use the account controls in section 2A; it will stay quiet while either two-sided desk budget is empty. Another Active account needs Up & Down Opener on the same chain to place the first stake of an empty-round pair.
Press Save on Rules. Nothing on the Rules tab saves by itself.
Press Start. Read the sentence it answers with — if the account still cannot bet, it says why.
Turn the master switch on. Until it is on, nothing is staked by any account.
Ticking a product is not enough

Up & Down on with no chain ticked, or Polls on with no category ticked, reaches nothing. Save on Rules and Start refuse that setup and explain what is missing. Always tick something underneath.

2 · The three ways an account enters a market

ModeWhat it doesWhen it is useful
CounterAnswers a player's stake by taking the other side of the same market, a short while after they bet.Keeps both sides of a market moving once players are already betting.
FillEvens up a thin side shortly before a market closes.Stops a market finishing badly one-sided.
OpenerPlaces the first bet on a market nobody has bet on yet.Gives players something to join. This is the only mode that acts on an empty market.
If nothing is happening, check Opener first

Counter needs a player to answer, and Fill needs a side to have money already. On a quiet market they correctly do nothing. Opener starts things off, so an empty desk with only Counter and Fill enabled is working as told.

How the desk shares markets between accounts

For opening or filling a market, the desk draws one account at random from those that could act. The draw is weighted: an account that has waited longer since its last bet is more likely to be drawn. The two-sided options below can then let a second account take the opposite side under separate limits.

What is randomHow
Which account betsDrawn for every market, weighted by how long each account has waited. An account that has just been given a market is very unlikely to be drawn again straight away.
An Opener's sideA fair coin flip, made once for each market and never repeated, so it cannot be re-drawn until a preferred side comes up.
Timing and amountsEvery wait before a bet is drawn from the range you set, amounts vary by the jitter you set, and a Counter answers only as often as its react chance allows.

A Counter always takes the side opposite the player it answers, and a Fill always tops up the thinner side — that is what they are for. None of this tells anyone anything about the result: the desk never sees a result before a market settles.

"Passed on" — a market handed to the next account

If the account drawn for a market cannot bet when the moment comes — its own balance floor, its gap between bets, its schedule, or another of its own limits stops it — the market is not lost. The desk passes it to another account that has not tried it yet (up to four accounts in all). The activity tables show the first account's row as Passed on. It is normal and needs no action; if one account is passed over often, look at its limits and its balance.

2A · Two-sided play is off by default

Two accounts may take opposite sides, with one account on each side and a different holder for each account. A desk stake never triggers another desk account's Counter answer. Opening, filling and stakes entered by an officer still keep the whole-market hold. Pairing may be an Up & Down account's only entry route if it covers a chain and all three account controls below are on.

OptionWhen it can actAccount setting
Answer a playerA player joins the side that another desk account holds. A second account may answer the player's stake on the unheld side; its amount is based only on the player's money. On that second account's Rules tab, turn on Answer on the other side. Counter and the account's usual scope and limits still apply. Both desk-wide two-sided budgets must also be set.
Pair a round with no player stakeOne desk account has placed an automatic Opener stake and no player has staked on either side. A second account may take the opposite side. Polls are not paired. On the second account's Rules tab, turn on Answer on the other side, set Pair rounds · share of other stake above 0%, and set Pair rounds per day above zero. The pair amount is the share of the first account's total open stake on one side, rounded down and never larger than it.
The two-sided desk budget starts empty

On Limits, Two-sided desk bets per day and Two-sided desk TZS per day must both be set before another desk account can take the opposite side, whether answering a player or pairing a round with no player stake. Both kinds use the same EAT-day count and stake budget. Leaving either field empty stops new second desk legs, including queued pairs and Counters. A bet already past the budget check inside its lock may finish. The master switch still stops all desk betting.

When you choose to allow two-sided play

Start with a small desk budget, for example 50 bets and TZS 50,000 per EAT day. Keep Up & Down Opener on for at least one Active account covering that chain, so it can place the first stake of an empty-round pair. Set the account switch and numbers above only for accounts you choose. Allow for the extra stakes in the desk's daily stake and bets-per-day limits, and keep fees well under its daily loss limit: reaching the realised loss stop turns the whole desk off. Review every suspicious-bet flag on a desk account; if the investigation confirms two desk accounts paired, send the evidence to the owner before closing it. A flag involving a player is never cleared as an authorised desk pair.

Recommended starting settings for Up & Down

Up & Down rounds are short, and the price the desk decides on goes stale within a few minutes, so these values keep the desk inside the round:

SettingSet it toWhy
Opener wait, maximum (Rules)45 secondsA longer wait lets the price go stale before the bet, and the bet is then refused. The desk now shortens the wait by itself when it must.
Gap between bets (Rules)20–30 secondsShort enough for an account to take part in several rounds a minute across chains, long enough to look natural.
Bets per market (Rules)2 or moreRoom for an Opener and a later answer to a player on the same round.
Bets per minute (Limits)12Most rounds open together on the minute. A lower limit makes the desk skip rounds at those moments.
Bets per day (Limits)1,000Counted over the last 24 hours, not since midnight. Below about 500 the desk runs out part-way through the day.
Balance (each holder)Well above the balance floorAn account that would go below its floor is passed over until it is topped up.

5 · An account is Active but not betting

Ask the desk first. On the account's own page, beside "Holder's transactions", there is a link that reads "Why is it not staking?". Press it and the desk checks the markets that are open right now, exactly the way it does when it plans opening and filling, and tells you in plain words what it found — for example "51 markets looked at (25 to open and 26 to fill), and this account would stake on 1 of them right now", or the reasons it refused, commonest first. It changes nothing and stakes nothing; it only reports. If you have changed a setting, press it again and the answer changes with it. Pairing is outside this sample.

It may also answer that the account could stake, but another account is more likely to be drawn first. That is not a fault: markets are drawn at random between the accounts that can take them, the longest-waiting most likely, so this account gets its share of the next markets that open. Nothing needs changing.

The list below is what to check if that answer does not make the cause obvious. The account's own row on the roster, and the panel beside the Rules form, will usually name the reason too.

CheckWhy it stops the account
Is the master switch on?With it off, nothing is staked by any account, however healthy each one looks.
Is a chain or category ticked?A ticked product with nothing ticked underneath reaches no market at all. This is the most common cause.
Is its entry mode on?Counter, Fill and Opener each need their own tick. A pair-only Up & Down account uses the controls in section 2A and is outside this opening-and-filling check.
Is every core money limit filled in?An empty core limit refuses bets. The two optional two-sided desk budgets may stay empty to prevent a second desk account taking the opposite side.
Does the account hold enough money?Its balance must stay above the balance floor after the stake, or the bet is refused.
Is a pool ceiling set to 0?A pool maximum of 0 means "only bet when the pool holds nothing", which a Counter can never see. Leave it empty for no ceiling.
Is it inside its schedule?Outside the days and hours you chose, it waits.
Has it already hit one of its limits?Its row shows what it has used of each limit. A money limit (daily stake, daily loss) starts again at midnight, East Africa time; a bet count (per hour, per day) is counted over the last hour or the last 24 hours, so room comes back as its oldest bets drop out.
Are there markets open at all?Up & Down chains must be running, and only Opener acts on a market with no bets yet.

6 · Did it win or lose?

Every bet an account places ends in one of four states. You can see them on the market itself and on the desk's Activity tab; what they came to in money over the last 7 days is on the desk's Results tab (below); and the ordinary money reports count house accounts as normal players, which is deliberate.

ResultWhat happenedThe money
OpenThe market has not finished yet.The stake is committed and counts towards open exposure.
WonThe market finished on the side the account took.The stake comes back with its winnings, less the platform's usual charges.
LostThe market finished on the other side.The stake is gone. This is what the daily loss limit counts.
VoidNobody took the other side, so there was no real contest.The whole stake comes back as a refund. Neither a win nor a loss.

A row that never became a bet shows why instead: Skipped or Expired (the moment passed or the market changed), Cancelled (an officer or a stop cancelled it), or Passed on (the account's own limits stopped it, and the market went to another account — see section 2).

The desk's Activity tab shows the same word in its Outcome column. Each row there is one round: Opening is what the account held before that stake left, Stake is the bet, and Closing is what it held right after — so Opening less Stake is always Closing. A later payout or refund does not change that row.

Expect refunds on quiet markets — they are not a fault

An Opener places the first bet on an empty market. If no player joins before it closes, the market is one-sided and everyone is refunded. That is the system working correctly: the account risked nothing and lost nothing. A run of refunds means the markets were quiet, not that anything is broken.

Where to read the result

The desk's Results tab shows what the desk's finished stakes came to over the last 7 days (EAT), in words — Profit TZS 12,600, Loss TZS 12,600 or Even, never a plus or minus sign. By day has one line per day for the whole desk, with how many stakes were placed; By account has each account's result today and over the 7 days. A stake counts on the day it was placed — the same day the daily loss limit counts it on — and a refund counts as nothing. A stake still running counts nothing until it finishes, so its day reads Still running, and can still change after midnight, until the last stake finishes and the day reads Final.

On the Limits tab, and on each account's own page, the daily loss limit has two rows against one cap. Projected is what could still be lost — your open stakes counted as though they all lost, which is what decides whether a new bet is allowed. Settled is what has actually finished: it reads used TZS 4,000 of TZS 100,000 when the day is down, and ahead by TZS 186 · limit TZS 100,000 when it is up. It is the same figure the Results tab shows for today — the whole desk's on the Limits tab, the account's own on its page.

The rest of the desk shows risk on purpose — staked today, what may still be lost, how close each limit is. For anything older than 7 days, use the ordinary money reports, where house accounts are counted exactly like any other player.

7 · Money limits

On the Limits tab, at two levels: limits on one account, and desk-wide limits that no account may exceed between them. The tighter of the two always wins.

An empty limit refuses; it does not permit

Leaving a core money limit blank does not mean "no limit" — the desk refuses the bet until it is set. Fill the core limits before starting. The two optional two-sided desk budgets are deliberately empty on release; leave either empty to prevent shared Counters and empty-round pairs.

How many accounts, and how fast

Max designated bots can be anything from 1 to 100. Set it to the number of accounts you actually run — the desk refuses a new account beyond it. Bets per minute can be anything from 1 to 120 for the whole desk; 12 is the recommended start. Raise it if the desk skips rounds at the start of each minute, when most rounds open together.

8 · The master switch

One switch governs the whole desk. Turning it off stops every account immediately; bets already placed stand and settle normally. Turning it on or off is recorded with who did it and why.

Safe to use

Turning the switch off is the safest thing you can do if anything looks wrong. It stops new bets at once and changes nothing that has already happened.

9 · Reading the desk's tables

Every table on the desk works the same way.

Sort by pressing a column's heading. Press it to order the table by that column; press it again to reverse the order. The heading in force shows an arrow (↑ or ↓). Running figures — Opening, Closing and Left today — and a few descriptive columns do not sort, because their order only makes sense in time.
Twenty rows to a page. Every list that can grow — activity, history, targets, the list of accounts — shows twenty rows with a pager under it. The desk can now hold up to 100 accounts, so the roster and the Results tab's By account table page the same way once there are more than twenty; the pager appears only when there is a second page.
A press that is loading says so. When you press a tab, a heading, a page number or a filter, a thin moving bar appears under what you pressed and the part of the page it will replace fades, until the new page arrives. On a slow connection, wait for it — nothing is stuck.
On a phone

The two activity tables show each stake as a stacked card with no heading row, so on a phone they carry a Sort · Panga row above the first card instead: one button for each column you can sort by, the one in use marked with its arrow (↑ or ↓). Press a button to sort by that column; press the marked one again to reverse the order. It does exactly what pressing that column's heading does on a computer, and the line above it still says which order you are looking at. Every other table keeps its heading row on a phone and sorts from it.
