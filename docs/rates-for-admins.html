50pick — how rates and payouts are calculated

A guide for administrators. Covers the two pari-mutuel games — normal polls and Up & Down — and Maswali Millionea, the weekly jackpot (section 5), which is not on sale yet.

50pick does not set odds. Normal polls and Up & Down are pari-mutuel: the players' own money makes the price. We take a fee from the losing side, and everyone on the winning side shares what is left, in proportion to what they staked. Maswali Millionea works differently — a fixed-price ticket and a weekly jackpot — and has a section of its own.

One rule, both pari-mutuel games. Up & Down and normal polls are charged in exactly the same way. The games differ in how the result is decided and how long they last — never in how the money is worked out.

Rates are frozen when a market is created. Changing a rate today affects new polls and new rounds only — never a bet that has already been placed. Two markets running side by side can legitimately settle on different rates, so if a player disputes a payout, read the rates on their market.
Because the maximum is per bet, and players may place as many bets as they like, a player's total money on one market is not limited by that maximum. Their balance and the daily loss limit are what bound it.

2. How every payout is worked out

  1. The pool — all the money in the market: winning side + losing side.
  2. Our fee — 13% of the losing side.
  3. The net pool — pool minus fee. This is what gets shared out.
  4. Each payout — the player's stake ÷ the winning side × the net pool.

A payout always includes the stake. A player who stakes 60,000 and is paid 86,100 has made a profit of 26,100.

On normal polls and Up & Down, a winning bet is never paid less than it staked. The fee only ever comes out of the losing side, so the winners' own stakes are untouched — whatever the shape of the market. Even on a market as lopsided as 999,000 against 1,000, a winning 10,000 bet on the crowded side still pays 10,009. Maswali Millionea is the exception: a winning ticket's share can be smaller than the ticket's price (section 5).

4. Game two — Up & Down

The money works exactly as in section 2 — 13% of the losing side. What is different is that Up & Down asks two separate questions: the price band decides who wins, the pool decides how much. A player can be right about the direction and still be refunded, if the price did not move far enough.

Who wins — the band

When the round opens we freeze the price and set two boundaries around it:

  • Close at or above the upper boundary → UP wins.
  • Close at or below the lower boundary → DOWN wins.
  • Close anywhere in between → the round voids: every stake refunded in full, and we charge nothing.

Today the band is the asset's smallest meaningful move — on Bitcoin's 5-minute round that is four hundredths of a dollar — so in practice the round is simply “higher or lower” and refunds are rare. Gold's 15-minute round is the exception: it runs a wider 0.05% band, so a void there is normal.

5. Game three — Maswali Millionea

Not on sale yet. Millionea is switched off for every player until the Owner turns it on, and no week can open until the Owner has recorded the licence basis and funded the guarantee. The figures below are its rules' defaults. Each week freezes its own at its open, and the week's page shows them — if a player disputes a figure, read their week's.

Millionea is not pari-mutuel. A player buys a ticket at a fixed price and answers fifteen YES/NO questions — ten main questions and five reserves. Only ten are graded. Every ticket with 10 of 10 shares the week's jackpot equally; if nobody gets 10 of 10, the whole week's sales roll into the next week's jackpot.

How a week's money is worked out

  1. The jackpot — the carry-over plus the week's sales, or the floor, whichever is larger.
  2. Our fee — 13% of the jackpot, only if at least one ticket has 10 of 10.
  3. Each share — the jackpot minus the fee, divided equally among the winning tickets, rounded down to the shilling. A player with two winning tickets gets two shares.
  4. The top-up — when the week's own money (the carry-over plus the sales) is less than the fee plus the shares, the guarantee pays the difference. Only in a week with a winner.
  5. What is left over — the few shillings an equal split cannot share. They join the next jackpot as far as the week's own money covers them; the rest stays with the guarantee, whose top-up covers only the fee and the shares — so in a week the guarantee tops up, the whole remainder stays with it.
A winning ticket can be paid less than it cost. There is no minimum share. In a 1,500-ticket week where 1,400 tickets reach 10 of 10, the jackpot is 1,500,000, our fee 195,000, and each share 1,305,000 ÷ 1,400 = 932 — less than the 1,000 the ticket cost; the 200 shillings the split cannot share join the next jackpot. The player is told this in the rules, in Help and by the assistant.

Example 4 — a week that voids whole

Six of the ten main questions void and only four reserves can stand in. Every ticket is refunded its 1,000 in full; no fee is taken and no top-up paid; a carried jackpot of, say, 1,100,000 carries on untouched to the next week.

Example 5 — the final week

If the Owner stops the product, the final week runs normally. If no ticket gets 10 of 10, the highest score shares the whole jackpot — no floor, no top-up, and our fee applies. Carried 1,800,000 plus 800 tickets: a jackpot of 2,600,000, our fee 338,000, and the four tickets with the best score (9 of 10) share 2,262,000 — 565,500 each.

The guarantee is funded before a week opens. It must hold the floor, less any confirmed carry-over, for every week not yet settled — the new one included. Week N+1 opens while week N is still being graded, so before week 1 the Owner pays in TZS 2,000,000. A settlement whose top-up the guarantee cannot cover stops and waits; it never pays less.
