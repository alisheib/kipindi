50pick — how rates and payouts are calculated

A guide for administrators. Covers both games: normal polls and Up & Down.

50pick does not set odds. Both games are pari-mutuel: the players' own money makes the price. We take a fee from the losing side, and everyone on the winning side shares what is left, in proportion to what they staked.

One rule, both games. Up & Down and normal polls are charged in exactly the same way. The games differ in how the result is decided and how long they last — never in how the money is worked out.

Rates are frozen when a market is created. Changing a rate today affects new polls and new rounds only — never a bet that has already been placed. Two markets running side by side can legitimately settle on different rates, so if a player disputes a payout, read the rates on their market.
Because the maximum is per bet, and players may place as many bets as they like, a player's total money on one market is not limited by that maximum. Their balance and the daily loss limit are what bound it.

2. How every payout is worked out

  1. The pool — all the money in the market: winning side + losing side.
  2. Our fee — 13% of the losing side.
  3. The net pool — pool minus fee. This is what gets shared out.
  4. Each payout — the player's stake ÷ the winning side × the net pool.

A payout always includes the stake. A player who stakes 60,000 and is paid 86,100 has made a profit of 26,100.

A winning bet is never paid less than it staked. The fee only ever comes out of the losing side, so the winners' own stakes are untouched — whatever the shape of the market. Even on a market as lopsided as 999,000 against 1,000, a winning 10,000 bet on the crowded side still pays 10,009.

4. Game two — Up & Down

The money works exactly as in section 2 — 13% of the losing side. What is different is that Up & Down asks two separate questions: the price band decides who wins, the pool decides how much. A player can be right about the direction and still be refunded, if the price did not move far enough.

Who wins — the band

When the round opens we freeze the price and set two boundaries around it:

  • Close at or above the upper boundary → UP wins.
  • Close at or below the lower boundary → DOWN wins.
  • Close anywhere in between → the round voids: every stake refunded in full, and we charge nothing.

Today the band is the asset's smallest meaningful move — on Bitcoin's 5-minute round that is four hundredths of a dollar — so in practice the round is simply “higher or lower” and refunds are rare. Gold's 15-minute round is the exception: it runs a wider 0.05% band, so a void there is normal.
