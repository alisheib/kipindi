Markets — Admin Runbook

50pick · 50pick.tz · where a market comes from, how it is resolved, how the money moves, and what the player sees. Every screenshot and every figure in this document was taken from the live platform, as the role named in its caption.

A market is one YES/NO question with a public source, a written criterion and a deadline. Players back a side; the two stakes form one pool; when the question is answered an officer seals the verdict and the pool pays the winning side. Nobody sets odds — the price you see is the crowd's own money, and the payout is arithmetic on the pool.

This is the companion to the Up & Down runbook, not a replacement. Up & Down is the fast game: a chain emits price rounds automatically and settles itself against a feed. Markets are the long-form game: a human writes the question and a human seals the answer. They share the same wallet, the same ledger and the same settlement code — and they are deliberately separate portfolios for the player.

Who can do what

50pick separates seeing a page from using a control on it. Both are enforced on the server, so a screen that hides a button is not the security boundary — it is the courtesy. The table is the live mapping, read from roles.ts and control-gates.ts.

Four of those are deliberately tighter than the page that hosts them. Resolving is trading work, but relaxing the two-admin rule and pulling a live market move money or change a control, so they are compliance. Approving a proposal pays the proposer a bonus, so it is growth. If a control is visible but locked, that is the system telling you the truth: you can see this page, you may not fire this lever.

Part 1 · Where a market comes from

There are four doors, and they all end in the same place — a row in the curation queue. Only the first is fully manual.

DoorWhat it is
New marketYou write the question. Four steps, below.
AI poll generationThe AI drafts a question with a source and a criterion; an officer reviews it and publishes. It never goes live unreviewed.
Player proposalsA player suggests a market. Approving one pays them a bonus, which is why approval is a growth control.
AI candidatesIdeas harvested from the event calendar, waiting to be promoted into drafts.

Writing one by hand — four steps

Sidebar → Curation queue → “New market” (top right)

Step 1 · The question Trading

Title in English, and optionally Swahili and Chinese. Then the category.

The category is not a label — it decides which sources you are allowed to cite. The trusted-source allowlist is per category. Writing this runbook, a real market was created with the correct source URL and the default category, and the platform refused it outright (figure below). That refusal is the guard working: it is the same rule that stops a market resolving against a page nobody approved.

Step 2 · The source and the deadline Trading

A public URL that both officers and players will resolve against, and the resolution timestamp.

The timestamp is entered in East Africa time and stored in UTC. The platform's timezone is Africa/Dar_es_Salaam. Type the time you mean locally; 03:00 on the form became 00:00 UTC in the database, verified on the market created for this guide.

Step 3 · The criterion Trading

At least 30 characters, and this is the text a resolver and a player are both held to. Write it so that two people reading it separately reach the same verdict.

Name the void condition too. A criterion that only says when YES and NO happen leaves the third case — the source went down, the figure was never published — to whoever finds it later. The worked example at the end of this guide states its own void condition in one sentence.

Step 4 · Review, then publish Trading

Everything on one card before it goes live. The banner above it states the fee model that will be frozen into this market at creation and cannot change afterwards, even if the platform's rates change tomorrow.

Part 2 · The money, before anyone touches it

These are the platform's live settings. They are frozen into each market when it is created.

SettingLive valueWhat it means
Fee modelloser-shareThe fee comes out of the losing pool. Winners are never charged.
Commission13%Of the pool — but capped, see below.
Fee ceiling33.3%Of the smaller side. The fee is the LOWER of the two.
Minimum stakeTZS 500Per bet.
Maximum stakeTZS 1,000,000Per bet.
Free exit5 minutesCash out free within 5 minutes of placing.
Cash-out fee0%After the free window.
Two-admin authorizationoffOne officer resolves in one action.
Why the cap exists. On a lopsided market 13% of the whole pool can exceed the entire losing side — the platform would be taking money that only the winners put in. fee = min(13% × pool, 33.3% × smaller side) makes that impossible. On a one-sided market there is no losing side at all, so the fee is nothing and every stake is refunded in full.

Part 3 · Resolving

Sidebar → Resolver queue

A market appears here when its resolution time passes. Read the card top to bottom: how late it is, how much player money it is holding, which way the crowd went, and then the three verdicts.

Reading the card

What you seeWhat it means
1D OVERDUEHow long past its resolution time. It scales its own unit — minutes, hours, days, weeks. The longer it waits the bigger the number gets, not smaller.
TZS 59,450 heldReal player money frozen in this pool until you act. This is the number that should decide what you pick up first.
The crowd dial + barHow the money is split. It is information, not evidence — resolve against the source, never against the crowd.
Source ↗The URL this market must be resolved from.

The three verdicts

VerdictWhat happens to the money
Resolve YES / NOThe winning side splits the whole pool less the capped fee. Losing stakes are gone.
VoidEvery stake is refunded in full, both sides, no fee. Use it when the question cannot be answered from its own source.
Resolving is not paying. Sealing a verdict adjudicates the market; the pool stays whole and every position stays open until settlement runs. That gap is deliberate — it is the window in which an upheld objection can still change the outcome. Once settlement runs, the money is in players' wallets and cannot be clawed back.

Two controls that are not yours Compliance

“Re-check this market now” spends one paid AI call and can seal a verdict, so it stays a compliance decision even though it sits on a trading page. Two-admin authorization is the toggle that restores the classic two-officer ceremony; relaxing it is a compliance control by design. Both appear locked to a trading officer rather than hidden — you can see that they exist and who to ask.

Part 4 · Settlement — how the money actually leaves the pool

Sidebar → Settlement Accounting

Settlement is normally automatic: when a verdict is sealed, that market gets its own timer set to the close of its objection window and pays itself then, provided no objection stands. This page is the human fallback — settle by hand, or see what an objection is freezing.

CounterWhat it means
Ready to settleVerdict sealed, objection window closed, waiting to pay.
Awaiting windowSealed, but the objection window is still open.
Frozen by objectionSomeone has objected. It will not pay until that is ruled on.
Timers armedMarkets that will settle themselves, and when the next one fires.
Settling is irreversible. Rule on every objection first. Once the money is in players' wallets it cannot be recovered.

Who can see this page, and who can run it

Compliance officers can view settlement — they hold accounting view — but running a settlement needs accounting act, which only finance and the owner hold. Seeing the page is not being able to fire it.

Part 5 · When something is wrong

SituationWhat to do
The source never published the figureVoid. Everyone gets their stake back. This is the honest answer, not a failure.
A LIVE market is wrong or unsafeEmergency void from the curation queue Compliance. It pulls the market and refunds every open stake.
A player disputes a verdictIt appears in Objections Compliance and freezes settlement until ruled on.
A market is overdue and holding moneyWork the resolver queue by the TZS … held pill, biggest first. Overdue money is the only truly urgent thing in this console.
Everything looks fine but nothing resolvesCheck the AI sentinel — it auto-resolves settled markets on a schedule and can be paused. A paused sentinel means every market waits for a human.

Part 6 · What the player sees

Everything above produces exactly three things for the player: a market they can back, a position they can watch, and a message when it is over.

Long-form markets message the player at every step — bet placed, betting closed with the exact payout if they are right, and the win or loss when it settles. Up & Down does not, and that is deliberate: a player running twenty 5-minute rounds an hour would get forty emails. Those rounds are collected into one daily digest instead — one message a day stating the wins, the losses and the refunds separately, with their own figures. If a player asks why they got nothing after an Up & Down round, that is the answer, and the digest arrives the next morning.

Part 7 · A market, start to finish — the worked example

Every figure below is from one real market created on production while writing this runbook.

market mkt_54f75a1959cdee5f1ed8 question Will Bitcoin trade above US$63,000 at 03:00 EAT on 3 August 2026? category crypto source api.twelvedata.com/quote created 2026-08-02 23:27:48 UTC resolves 2026-08-03 00:00:00 UTC bets alpha YES TZS 2,000 echo NO TZS 2,000 pool TZS 4,000

Creating it — and being refused

The first attempt was rejected outright, and correctly: the source domain was approved under crypto while the form still carried the default category sports. That is the figure in Part 1. Fixing the category published it in one click.

Resolving it

At the instant the criterion names — 00:00:00 UTC on 3 August 2026 — the platform's own reader was pointed at the market's own declared source:

BTC/USD 63,570.00 provider-quoted 2026-08-03T00:00:00.000Z skew 19s (limit 90s) → strictly above 63,000 → YES

The ceremony page also carried an independent opinion: the AI sentinel returned YES at 88% confidence, citing Bybit $63,381, CoinGecko $63,254.75, Yahoo $63,043.63 and CoinDesk $63,454.69 — and the platform labelled that recommendation “NOT THE APPROVED SOURCE” in red, because none of those four is the domain this market declared. The two agreed, and the verdict was recorded against the approved one.

Sealing is not paying, and the worked example proves it. Immediately after sealing, the database read: market RESOLVED, outcome YES, settledAt still null, and both positions still OPEN with both wallets unchanged. The page said the same thing — objection window 23h, closes 4 Aug 2026 03:08, payouts are provisional until the 24-hour window elapses. Do not read “verdict sealed” as “the players were paid”; check the wallet, not the toast.
Dated note, 2026-09-05 — the window is now 1 hour, not 24. The quotation above is a verbatim reading of the officer's screen on the day this walkthrough was captured, and it matches the photograph beside it, so its numbers are left exactly as they were. What has changed since is the setting: management shortened the objection window from 24 hours to 1, and every surface now reads it from configuration rather than stating it. immediately after sealing, read from the live database: market RESOLVED · YES · settledAt = null alpha YES 2,000 → OPEN, payout null, wallet 57,450 (unchanged) echo NO 2,000 → OPEN, payout null, wallet 25,400 (unchanged) objection window closes 4 Aug 2026 03:08 EAT — payout is armed for then

When that window closes with nothing standing, the market settles itself: the YES side takes the TZS 4,000 pool less the fee, and the NO stake is gone. This market is frozen at the loser-share fee model, so the fee is a slice of the losing side only — (3% platform + 10% operator) × the NO pool = 13% × 2,000 = TZS 260. That leaves a net pool of TZS 3,740, and alpha — the only YES predictor — receives all of it: TZS 3,740, a profit of TZS 1,740 on a TZS 2,000 stake.

⛔ Never assume which fee model a market settles at — read it off that market's own page. There are two, they produce different money on the same pools, and both are live right now. Counted on production on 3 August 2026, of the 19 long-form polls currently open, 7 settle at loser-share and 12 at capped-commission — so the model this worked example uses is the minority case, and guessing gets the majority of the board wrong.

Part 8 · The checklist

  1. Before creating: is the source domain approved under this market's category? Is the criterion one two people would read the same way? Does it say what happens if the source is silent?
  2. Every session: open the resolver queue and sort your attention by TZS … held, not by how old the card looks.
  3. Before resolving: open the source link. Resolve from it, never from the crowd bar.
  4. Before settling: is anything frozen by an objection? Settling is irreversible.
  5. If nothing is resolving at all: check the sentinel is not paused before suspecting the markets.
