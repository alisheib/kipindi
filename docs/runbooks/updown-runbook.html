Up & Down — Operator & Player Runbook

50pick · 50pick.tz · how a round is generated, how it is resolved against Twelve Data, and how a player plays it. Every screenshot in this document is from the live platform.

Up & Down asks one question: will the price be higher or lower when the clock runs out? A chain is one asset at one duration. Once started it emits a round at every clock boundary, back to back, with no further operator action. Nobody types a price and nobody decides an outcome — the price comes from Twelve Data and the outcome is arithmetic.

The navigation

Part 1 · Generating rounds

Five steps, in this order. Steps 1–3 are once per asset. Steps 4–5 are what you repeat.

Step 1 · Approve the price source domain Trading

Sidebar → Sources & categories

Add twelvedata.com and enable it. An asset can only point at a domain that is an enabled trusted source, and this is checked three times: when the asset is added, when it is enabled, and again every time a chain starts.

The allowlist is per category. One twelvedata.com row does not cover both a crypto asset and a macro one — add it under each category you will use, or the chain refuses to start.

Step 2 · Add the asset Owner

Sidebar → Up & Down → Overview → “+ Add asset” (top right)

You now make only two choices. Pick an asset class, then a symbol. Everything the symbol determines — category, names in all three languages, icon, decimals, minimum move and the price-source URL — is filled in and locked, and shown to you in a “set by the symbol” panel so you can check it.

ChoiceWhat it means
Asset classCrypto · Metals · Foreign exchange · Indices. This filters the symbol list.
SymbolTwelve Data’s own symbol, e.g. BTC/USD, XAU/USD. Only symbols the platform can actually quote are offered.
KeyYours, and never renamed afterwards. Pre-filled with a sensible suggestion.
The category is no longer yours to choose, and that is deliberate. It selects the trading calendar: crypto is 24/7, everything else follows the FX/metals week. On 2 August 2026 a coin was added as macro and the console then showed it “closed · opens 22:00 UTC” every weekend, for no reason. On the same afternoon an asset was added with the symbol ETH instead of ETH/USD, pointed at a page the feed cannot read — it voided 27 of 27 rounds. Neither was rejected at the time. Both are now impossible to create, in the form and on the server.

Before you enable it: press “Check the live feed”

The form asks the real provider, through the same two functions that settle money, and tells you one of four things:

VerdictWhat to do
Would confirmGood. It shows the price, the provider’s own quote time and the skew in seconds. Enable it.
Readable but too slowDo not arm it. The quote is older than the staleness window, so a round cannot confirm its opening price and every round will void and refund while looking perfectly healthy. This is exactly what SOL/USD did — 100% void, 8 of 8 — because its quote lags about 132 s against a 90 s window.
Market is shutCome back when it reopens. A quote read while the market is closed proves nothing. The panel tells you the reopening time.
Cannot be quotedThe provider does not carry this symbol on the current plan. Pick another.

Then switch the asset to Enabled. A disabled asset cannot emit rounds.

Metals & FXOpens Sunday 22:00 UTC (Monday 01:00 EAT), closes Friday 21:00 UTC (Saturday 00:00 EAT). Shut all Saturday. It runs continuously in between — there is no daily close.
While a market is shut the platform refuses to open or read a round, so you will see no results at all until it reopens. That is deliberate: the provider keeps quoting a shut market with a fresh-looking timestamp, and settling real money on a weekend gold print would pay out on a price no market ever made. The Overview’s Market column says closed · opens 22:00 UTC whenever this applies.

Step 3 · Set the reading method Owner

Up & Down → Overview → Price reading method

Set method feed and provider twelvedata.

The other provider, mock, is a simulator. It refuses by construction in production — it will never settle a real round. If every round is voiding, check this card first.

Step 4 · Create the chain Trading

Up & Down → Overview → Chains card → “+ Add chain”
  • Asset and Duration — 5, 15 or 30 minutes.
  • Margin % — leave blank. Blank inherits the measured value for that asset class and duration, and the placeholder shows you what that is.
  • Min / max stake — blank inherits the platform defaults.

A new chain starts stopped. Creating it emits nothing.

Step 5 · Start it Trading

Chains card → the chain’s row → Start

The row then shows RUNNING and a Next boundary time. From that moment rounds appear on their own.

Pause stops new rounds and keeps the chain’s clock. Stop stops new rounds entirely. Both let rounds already open finish and settle normally — no player is ever left holding an unsettled stake.

Part 2 · How resolution works

This is the part to understand before testing, because a round that refunds is not a round that failed.

One price per boundary, shared

At every clock boundary the engine reads the price once for that asset and that instant, from api.twelvedata.com/quote. That single reading is written to a write-once ledger and shared by every round edge on that instant. So:

  • Round N’s close is literally round N+1’s open — the same number, to the digit. Adjacent rounds cannot disagree.
  • A reading, once confirmed, cannot be overwritten by a retry or a second server.

The freshness rule

Twelve Data reports when it last quoted the price. If that time is more than 90 seconds from the round boundary, the reading is refused — we never settle on a price we cannot date to the moment the round ended. A refused boundary is retried on a backoff ladder; if it never confirms, the round voids and every stake is refunded in full.

The winning band (the margin)

At open, the round freezes two targets: open + margin and open − margin, where margin is a percentage of the opening price. At close:

Close priceOutcome
at or above the UP targetUP — Up backers win
at or below the DOWN targetDOWN — Down backers win
anywhere betweenVOID — every stake refunded in full, no fee

The band is frozen at open, so changing the margin later only affects future rounds. A bet already taken can never be re-priced.

The margin is the one setting that decides whether the game feels alive. Too wide and almost every round refunds; too narrow and a one-cent flicker decides real money. The defaults were measured against roughly a thousand real windows per duration and land at about one round in three refunding: 0.02% at 5 minutes, 0.03% at 15, 0.05% at 30. Leave the field blank unless you are deliberately tuning one asset.

Market hours

Crypto trades 24/7. Metals, FX and indices do not: their week runs Sunday 22:00 UTC to Friday 21:00 UTC. Outside that window no round opens and none settles, and the Chains card says closed · opens 22:00 UTC. This is deliberate — a data provider will still answer at the weekend, and settling real money on a market that is shut is not something the platform will do.

What a round pays

Winners split the pool after the platform’s commission — 13% of the pool, capped at a third of the smaller side. On a balanced TZS 10,000 pool that is TZS 1,300. Frozen onto each round at creation. The × 1.4 est. on the buttons is an estimate, never fixed odds, and the card says so.

Part 3 · Playing

Top navigation → Up & Down (/updown)
  1. Pick a stake — a chip, or Custom.
  2. Tap Up or Down.
There is no confirmation step on this game. The first tap places the bet and takes the money — that is the design for a five-minute round. Check the stake chip before tapping.

Betting closes at the boundary, because the bet is on the price at that instant.

The settlement proof

Every settled round carries an auditable record below the card: each price with its own source, the time the provider quoted it and the time we observed it, the outcome, and an excerpt of the raw provider response. A tester should not take our word for a result — this is the panel that lets them check it.

Top navigation → History (/updown/history)
Up & Down does not send a notification for each win or loss — a player running twelve rounds an hour would get twelve messages. The result is on the round card and in History. Money movements are always recorded in the wallet and on the statement.

Part 4 · Testing it, and reading a failure correctly

A test that proves the game works

  1. As Trading, start a 5-minute crypto chain (crypto so market hours cannot confuse the result).
  2. As two different players, back opposite sides of the same round.
  3. Wait for the boundary. Do nothing else — settlement is automatic.
  4. Check, in this order:
    • the round has an open price and a close price, and they differ;
    • the outcome matches the direction those two prices moved;
    • the winner’s wallet went up and the loser’s stayed down;
    • stake in − paid out = the commission.
Read the prices, never just the outcome flag. Two identical prices with a VOID means the feed gave the same number twice; two different prices with a VOID means the move was inside the band. Those look identical in an outcome column and mean completely different things.

Every round is voiding — what to check, in order

  1. Is the chain running? Chains card → State.
  2. Is the market open? Chains card → Market. A shut metals market opens nothing.
  3. Is the provider twelvedata, not mock? Price reading method card.
  4. Is the margin far too wide? Chains card → Void rate. A high void rate with healthy prices means the band, not the feed.
  5. Is the domain approved for that asset’s category? Sources & categories.
  6. Only then suspect the feed. Round detail shows the refusal reason in words.

The refusal reasons say different things and send you to different places: market closed is the calendar; stale means a price arrived but too far from the boundary; no-move means the price moved less than the band; unparseable or wrong source means the asset is pointing somewhere it should not be.

What a void is not

A void is not a loss and not an error. Every stake comes back in full and no commission is taken. It is the platform declining to decide, which is the correct behaviour whenever the price cannot be established or the move was too small to call.
